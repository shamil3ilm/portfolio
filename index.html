      Fraud review

      Shared bank accounts, visible at a glance

      BeforeThe screen staff use to vet new customers showed documents and risk scores, but not the bank accounts the customer had linked.

      AfterEvery linked account is listed with how it was verified (instant, micro-deposit, pending or manual) and how many other customers use the same account, one click from their details. Searchable by routing number, account number, phone or email.

      Scheduling

      Evening fraud-check files that never ran

      BeforeA daily Positive Pay file (the list banks use to catch forged checks) set for after about 8 PM US Eastern was matched against the next day in UTC, and silently skipped.

      AfterThe run time is now read against today's date in the account's own time zone, so evening runs go out on the right day.

      Clear errors

      A failure message that said the wrong thing

      Before"Your recurring payment of $500.00 failed due to insufficient funds." The payment money was fine; it was the mailing fee that could not be charged.

      After"…could not be processed because the $2.50 mailing fee could not be charged", plus which balance to top up. The same detail goes into the audit trail. (Example amounts.)

      Performance

      Big statements built in the background

      BeforeAccount statements were built while the customer waited on the page, however many transactions they held.

      AfterStatements over 1,000 transactions are built by a background job and emailed as PDF, CSV or Excel.

      Approvals

      Six real reasons instead of "failed"

      BeforeWhen a payment could not be approved, the approver got a generic failure and no way to know what to do next.

      AfterA specific reason every time, such as "You are not an approver for this payment" or "This payment is no longer awaiting approval", and a clear note when only part of a batch went through.
