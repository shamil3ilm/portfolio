      Delegated access · Featured

      Acting for customers, safely

      Payroll and bookkeeping providers can work in their customers' accounts without sharing logins, while payments waiting for the customer's own approvers stay theirs.

      ProblemPayroll and bookkeeping providers needed to work inside their customers' accounts through the existing payment APIs, without sharing logins and without being able to sign off the customer's own payments.

      ApproachAn authorization layer added to the existing APIs, so providers and customers go through the same rules. Access is granted by the customer, every call is attributed and audited, and payments that need the customer's own approvers stay theirs.

      Read the case study →Try the demo →
      • KeyAccess starts with a single-use, expiring key the customer creates
      • LogEvery call is attributed to the provider and recorded, allowed or refused
      • DenyEvery refusal looks the same from outside; the real reason goes to the audit log
      Payment approvals

      Approved means unchanged

      Money can't be sent to a different bank account once a payment is approved.

      ProblemIn a multi-level approval flow, a recipient's bank details could change after a payment was submitted, so approvers could sign off one destination while the money went to another.

      ApproachThe details that decide where money lands are fingerprinted at submission and re-checked at release. Recipient and bank-account edits are blocked while a payment is pending, and every refused approval returns a specific reason instead of a generic failure.

      Try the demo →
      Recurring payments

      Schedules that stop when they should

      Deleting a recipient or bank account now stops every payment scheduled to it.

      ProblemDeleting a recipient or bank account didn't stop the recurring payments tied to it, and cancelling several schedules at once left their upcoming payments queued.

      ApproachDeleting now warns first, then cancels every linked schedule with an audit note, as one all-or-nothing change. Bulk deletes go through the same per-record path, and bulk cancel clears every selected schedule.

      Read the case study →Try the demo →
      Payment imports

      Spreadsheets that map themselves

      Payment spreadsheets in any column layout import without matching every column by hand.

      ProblemCustomers upload payment spreadsheets in any column order and naming, sometimes without a header row, and had to match every column by hand before importing.

      ApproachContributed to layered column matching: header rules first, then the sample values themselves, then an AI model for whatever is still unmatched, with a position-based fallback. The user only reviews the result.

      AI + documents

      Payroll PDFs into checks

      Payroll PDFs become ready-to-print checks, created only once the user confirms.

      ProblemPayroll arrives as PDFs, and each page had to be typed in as a check by hand.

      ApproachWorked on payroll-PDF processing for a payroll-provider integration: splitting a merged PDF into one check per page, and creating the checks only after the user confirms them.

      AI observability · Featured

      Knowing what every AI call costs

      Every AI call's cost, speed and failures are visible, per feature and per model.

      ProblemAI usage arrived as one provider bill. Nobody could see which feature or model the cost came from, how often calls failed, or how slow they were.

      ApproachTracking built into the shared AI client: one record per request, written even when the call fails, with feature, model, tokens, cost and response time. A dashboard breaks it down by feature, model and provider account, with a 24-hour health view.

      Fraud review

      Shared bank accounts, visible at a glance

      BeforeThe screen staff use to vet new customers showed documents and risk scores, but not the bank accounts the customer had linked.

      AfterEvery linked account is listed with how it was verified (instant, micro-deposit, pending or manual) and how many other customers use the same account, one click from their details. Searchable by routing number, account number, phone or email.

      Scheduling

      Evening fraud-check files that never ran

      BeforeA daily Positive Pay file (the list banks use to catch forged checks) set for after about 8 PM US Eastern was matched against the next day in UTC, and silently skipped.

      AfterThe run time is now read against today's date in the account's own time zone, so evening runs go out on the right day.

      Clear errors

      A failure message that said the wrong thing

      Before"Your recurring payment of $500.00 failed due to insufficient funds." The payment money was fine; it was the mailing fee that could not be charged.

      After"…could not be processed because the $2.50 mailing fee could not be charged", plus which balance to top up. The same detail goes into the audit trail. (Example amounts.)

      Approvals

      A real reason instead of "failed"

      BeforeWhen a payment could not be approved, the approver got a generic failure and no way to know what to do next.

      AfterA specific reason every time, such as "You are not an approver for this payment" or "This payment is no longer awaiting approval", and a clear note when only part of a batch went through.

      Integrations

      Accounting and payroll integrations

      BeforeImports from third-party accounting software finished silently, and checks from a third-party payroll tool were created before the user had confirmed them.

      AfterImports of bills, expenses, refund receipts and checks now email the user when done and record who ran them; payroll checks are created only on confirmation.

      Incident recovery

      A storage outage that failed whole mailing batches

      BeforeWhen stored check PDFs became unreadable, checks sent in batches failed together: one missing file failed every check in its batch, so most of the failures were collateral. Nothing had been mailed.

      AfterEvery PDF was regenerated and checked for the right payee and amount, then each check was re-sent on its own. A status check plus the mailing provider's unique transaction ID meant no check could be mailed twice or with the wrong file.
