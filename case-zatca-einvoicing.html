Saudi e-invoicing on an existing business platform

Taxfly ↗, Netplex's billing, inventory and accounting platform, served customers in Qatar, Saudi Arabia and the UAE. Saudi customers needed invoices that meet ZATCA's e-invoicing rules, and I developed and investigated that workflow independently, up to the point I handed it over.

PHP Laravel Intern, Netplex SolutionsJun – Nov 2025Laravel · PHP · PostgreSQL

Starting with an existing system

Before changing anything, I mapped how the application already worked: its routes, controllers, models, Blade views, roles and database structure. That map fed into redesigning it into modules on Inertia, Vue, TypeScript and Vite.

Alongside that, I gathered requirements from new clients across Qatar, Saudi Arabia and the UAE and worked on the accounting workflows their businesses depend on.

The ZATCA workflow I worked on

ZATCA, the Saudi tax authority, requires electronic invoices in a set format, protected so they can't be quietly changed. The workflow covered:

  1. XML generation: turning the platform's invoices into the XML format ZATCA requires.
  2. Hashing and signing: fingerprinting each invoice and signing it, so any later change can be detected.
  3. QR codes: encoding the required invoice details into a QR code in ZATCA's TLV format.
  4. Certificate handling: managing the certificates the signatures depend on.
  5. Validation and compliance errors: checking invoices against the rules and tracing each compliance error back to its cause.

Where it stood at handover

It was not finished when I left. I developed and investigated the workflow up to that point and handed it over there. I'm describing the work I did, not a completed integration.

The lesson

A rejected invoice usually comes down to one wrong field or one byte that changed the hash, so most of the time goes into tracing an error message back to its exact cause. I later took the same standard further in my own project, Masaar.
