Case study · Payments reliability

Making scheduled payments stop when they should

Businesses schedule rent, payroll and supplier payments to repeat on their own. Two gaps meant a schedule could outlive the thing it was paying.

Software Engineer, Zil Money2026PHP · Laravel · MySQL

The problem

  • Deleting a recipient or bank account didn't stop its schedules. The next run would still try to pay a destination the customer had removed.
  • Cancelling several schedules at once only looked finished. The schedules showed as cancelled, but their upcoming payments stayed queued. No money moved, but the records said one thing and the queue said another.

What I built

  1. A plain warning before the delete. If the recipient or bank account still has active schedules, the user sees: "This recipient has active recurring or scheduled payments. Proceeding will cancel them. Do you want to continue?"
  2. On confirm, every schedule tied to it is cancelled, across every recurring payment type, from bank transfers to mailed checks. Deleting a bank account also stops payment-request forms that pay into it.
  3. Each cancellation says why. The schedule's history gets a note like "Auto-canceled: associated recipient 'Harbor Realty' was deleted", so support and the customer can see exactly what happened.
  4. The delete and the cancellations succeed or fail together. On these paths, if a cancellation fails, the delete is undone too, so a deleted recipient never keeps live schedules.
  5. Bulk actions got the same protection. Deleting many recipients at once used a shortcut that skipped the per-record safeguards, so I changed it to delete them one by one, only within the customer's own account. Bulk cancel now clears the upcoming payments of every selected schedule, and mailed checks that were held for a schedule are released for use again.

The lesson

Deleting one recipient was safe. Deleting several at once was not, because the bulk path skipped the code that runs for each record. Since then, whenever a safeguard runs per record, I check the bulk path too.

The safeguard covers every way the web app deletes them: recipients and bank accounts, one at a time and in bulk.
